# 55. One session is paused at a turn boundary and keeps its slot for a bounded time

Status: accepted, 2026-11-15. Spec #90, story 76 (ticket #128).

A person could steer or stop a session, or pause the whole mission (no new task starts; work in flight carries on). Holding one session while the rest of the mission works had no mechanism. The questions are what "paused" is for the attempt, how long it may hold its runner slot, and how the clocks that count working time and timeouts stay coherent.

## Decision
1. **Two commands, human only.** `session.pause` and `session.continue` (scope `session:steer`; a session's own credential is refused them, as for steer and stop). "Continue" is named so it cannot be mistaken for `session.resume`, which starts a new session from a checkpoint (ADR 0045). The mission state, and every other member's session, are not touched.
2. **The attempt stays alive.** A paused session keeps its job fence, lease (the runner goes on renewing it) and runner slot; its session state stays `running`. What a person sees ("pausing", "paused", "resuming") is a separate pause fact on the session view, so nothing that reads the session lifecycle changes meaning.
3. **At a turn boundary, taken by the engine.** The Governor records the request; the runner holding the attempt is told on every heartbeat until it reports; the engine holds when its turn is over and before the next request to the model, and the runner then reports `session-paused` as a progress event the Governor dates on its own clock. Pi holds in the vendor loop's own next-turn hook, so a steer sent while held is picked up right after and goes out in the first request after the continue. An engine advertises `pause` only if it can do this; the others show the control disabled with the reason. A model answer with no tool call has no boundary: the pause is not taken and the session finishes.
4. **Commands are level-triggered; stop is delivered until it has taken effect.** A pause or continue is the state of one `attempt_pauses` row, repeated by the heartbeat until the runner's report changes it; the runner applies each command id once. A stop row is no longer deleted when handed out but kept until the session ends, because a held session keeps renewing its lease and so no longer ends by lease expiry if a stop acknowledgement is lost. Steer stays at most once (advisory).
5. **Clocks.** Held time `[paused, resumed)` is cut out of mission working time (ADR 0016: the union of attempt intervals, each split around its held ranges); the step's timeout (ADR 0046) is not enforced while held and its deadline moves forward by the held time on continue; the runner's execution deadline stops counting while the engine is held. Money and tokens do not move (nothing is sent to the model).
6. **Hold limit.** A paused session may hold its slot for 30 minutes (`BRAIN_SESSION_PAUSE_HOLD_MINUTES`). Past it, the Governor's regular timer queues an ordinary stop with the reason; the attempt ends, the slot is freed, and the engine's saved conversation (Pi checkpoints a non-finished attempt) can be resumed from its checkpoint. The session view and the console show when it will stop.

## Limits (be honest)
- Pause takes effect at the engine's boundary, not at once: a long model turn or a running tool call finishes first, and the heartbeat adds up to 15 seconds.
- Only Pi advertises `pause`. Claude, ACP and OpenCode sessions, and the fixture engine, show it disabled.
- A held session occupies its slot and counts toward the mission's member cap; that is the price of "same session". Releasing the slot is what stop and resume-from-checkpoint are for.
- The runner reports held and released as progress events; a runner that never reports leaves the session "pausing" until a continue, a stop or the end of the attempt.
