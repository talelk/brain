# 69. An L3 evaluator is a person who did not do the work, and attests the evidence

Status: accepted, 2026-11-15. Builds on ADR 0012 (independence levels, a verdict is never a model's opinion), ADR 0023 (hosted checks) and ADR 0050 (a request to a person is a record, and Needs you derives it).

Independence L1 (a separate process) and L2 (a separate runner trust domain) are checked by a runner. L3, a human or external evaluator, was refused at admission as unsupported. A task whose result matters to someone other than its author had no way to require a person's judgement short of the mission-level sign-off (#92), which judges a whole mission, not one step's evidence.

## Decision
1. **An eval step of independence L3 waits for a person.** The level of a step is the higher of the step's and its evaluator release's. At L3 the Governor creates no job and no runner can claim one. It writes one `evaluation_attestations` row (the step, the pinned assignment with its bundle digests, criteria and required checks, and the attesters the step declared), sets the step to waiting, and suspends the run, as a gate does. The row, the step and the run change in one transaction, so a crash leaves a pending step the next pass raises again, never half a request.
2. **The request is a record, and it is open while no verdict exists.** Nothing stores "open": Needs you derives the blocking item `attestation` (tier 3, with questions and parked work) from the request, its run not having ended and no verdict on its bundles. It opens the attestation page whether or not a mission is behind it. A cancelled or failed run closes it.
3. **Who may attest is read now, never stored.** A step may declare `attesters` (a workspace role, named people, or either); with none, any workspace member who may do mission work. Whatever is declared, the people whose work it is are refused: whoever started the run, the owner of the mission it serves (read at attest time, so a transfer does not launder it), whoever started a delegation or a session behind it, and for a machine principal the person it acts for. The author can never be the L3 evaluator. Only a person can attest (the command is human-only), and the page tells an author why they cannot instead of offering a form that would be refused. The item is pushed to the people who may answer it, not to its authors.
4. **The attestation is a verdict.** `evaluation.attest` carries the request, the exact bundle digests the person reviewed (a stale review is refused), the outcome and one status with a note for every pinned check. The verdict is the same contract a runner's is: it names the pinned evaluator release as its oracle, binds each check's artifacts from the immutable bundle, and is accepted by the person, at independence L3. The same rules apply: every check once, the criterion the assignment names, `verified` only when every check passed, `rejected` only with a failure, `incomplete` only with a missing or unclear check (one shared function), reconciled effects before `verified`, artifacts re-hashed. It is recorded as the evaluator of the step: the verdict's evaluator id is the request.
5. **A verdict moves the work as a runner's does.** Verified concludes the author's attempt as succeeded and the run goes on; rejected or incomplete concludes it as failed, so the existing failure and send-back flow takes over. An incomplete verdict is treated as not verified because a verdict is immutable: waiting for a better one would strand the run, and the person who judged it incomplete wants a new attempt. Projection is idempotent and retried by the maintenance pass if a crash falls between the verdict and its effects.
6. **The trust read counts it as human.** What a person passed is human sign-off, never a deterministic tool's check; the evidence view says "Attested by <person> · independence L3 (a person)".
7. **No model decides anything.** Nothing here calls a model or ranks. The advisory judge, if any, stays a separate opinion.

## Limits
- A request has no timeout: a person may be slow, and a run waiting for one is a run waiting for a person, shown in Needs you until answered or cancelled.
- `attesters` names a role among those that may do mission work (operator, workspace lead) or people; "external" evaluators outside the workspace are not modelled.
- The verdict's evaluator id column holds the request id for an L3 verdict, since a person has no runner attempt.
